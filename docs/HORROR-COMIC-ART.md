# Pencil horror-comic art

The approved [moonlit court concept](../output/concepts/horror-comic-minimal-v3/01-moonlit-court-masked-vs-skeleton.png)
is the game's sole current background/player direction. Player 1 wears the
reference's ochre hat and coat, violet clothes, and ink-patterned face covering.
Player 2 has exposed cream bones, a skull, and plum shorts. The scene contains
blue pencil-colored sky, a cream moon, bare branches at the edges, a solid black
forest skyline with small crosses, and a violet ground plane. There are no hotel
buildings or resort scenery.

## Assets and rig

Built-in ImageGen generated the player atlases and pencil plates. Exact English
prompts are recorded in [asset-prompts.json](../output/horror-work/asset-prompts.json)
and [the detailed plate study prompt](../output/horror-work/detailed-background-prompt.json).
Source PNGs are preserved in `output/horror-work/sources/`.

- `public/art/horror/background-moonlit.webp`: the quieter original sky/ground
  plate from the requested gameplay reference, packaged at 2560 by 1600.
  Its generated source is 1586 by 992; packaging registers and resamples it to
  the larger production size. Scenery is independently drawn at device resolution.
- `public/art/horror/masked.webp`: thirteen masked-player cutouts in a 4-by-4 atlas.
- `public/art/horror/skeleton.webp`: thirteen skeleton cutouts with transparent rib gaps.
- `public/art/horror/props/*.webp`: nine transparent comic prop cutouts. The
  [manifest](../output/horror-work/props-manifest.json) lists every production
  file and its registered source crop. Built-in ImageGen generated the
  [source atlas](../output/horror-work/sources/props-atlas.png) using the
  [saved prompt](../output/horror-work/props-prompt.json); the
  [packaging script](../output/horror-work/package-props.mjs) preserves alpha.

Trees, distant forest, crosses and moon are generated by `src/render/horrorScenery.ts`.
The earlier `background.webp`, busier `background-detailed.webp`, and raster
`scenery.webp` are historical assets and are not loaded by the active game.

The [atlas packaging script](../output/horror-work/package-assets.mjs) registers
each sprite to its guide cell. The [moonlit plate packaging script](../output/horror-work/package-moonlit-background.mjs)
normalizes the painted sky/ground boundary to 84% of the source image.
`src/render/horrorAtlas.json` maps cells into original local body
coordinates. The hat and coat tails have expanded visual bounds; this never
changes collision shapes. All thirteen parts use the existing closed-joint
poses, individual rotations, size powers, and detached-part scales. No opaque
skin undercoat or human silhouette mask is used behind skeleton bones.

## Animation and paper texture

`HorrorScenery` builds a single solid forest skyline, bare branches entering
from the edges, and a cream lunar disc with ink crater patches and pencil
marks using seeded Canvas paths. It caches three contour variants at device
resolution, from 1280 to 3840 pixels wide. The scenery is clipped at the court's
drawn floor. Individual distant trees are removed: broad black masses form the
low horizon from the requested gameplay screenshot. Six small weathered crosses
protrude above that silhouette, with their bases hidden inside it.

Line boil is barely visible: at 5 Hz, only outline vertices move by at most
0.025 stage pixels per axis. Only the forest edge, foreground branches and moon
contour redraw. Crosses, lunar center, crater details,
bark marks and branch geometry stay fixed. This replaces the earlier raster displacement, which
moved internal texture. Blue sky, ground and collision boundaries remain fixed.

`PencilGrain` generates eight deterministic 256-pixel noise tiles once. A small
fixed soft-light pass supplies paper tooth; the dominant moving pass changes
tiles and offsets at 12 Hz. Each noise sample spans three CSS pixels, so the
coarser grain keeps the same visible size on ordinary and Retina displays.
Both opacities scale with the user's percentage:
0.1 times strength for fixed tooth and 0.52 times strength for animated grain,
where strength is normalized to 0–1. Frame rendering uses cached patterns and
two fills, without per-frame image readback, random-number generation, or a
WebGL dependency. Grain covers artwork before the HUD is painted, preserving
sharp interface text.

Grain intensity is fixed at 16% for both the court and its surrounding paper.
The main-menu and pause sliders, percentage readouts, and preference loading
have been removed. Earlier `rackdoll-grain-strength` values are ignored.
Painted pencil texture remains part of the underlying artwork.

The shared scenery clock stops while paused. Reduced motion fixes both the
line-boil frame and the moving grain pass. The same texture provides a
quiet matte surround outside the game canvas.

## Remaining game elements

`comicStyle.ts` supplies shared cream, ochre, mint, plum and rust spot colors,
fixed ink-panel contours, hatching and ragged impact bursts. The volleyball has
curved mint/cream panels, black seams, pencil scratches and a flat ownership
tint. Its actual flight history drives three narrow speed strokes instead of
additive neon bloom. Charge, stun, power, impact and scoring feedback use the
same ink and lettering treatment. None of these passes changes collisions.

The five existing hazards now appear as a haunted magnet, voodoo boxer, fire
skull, bone-jaw spring trap and ectoplasm. Only their displayed labels change;
the existing IDs, lifetimes, targeting, fists, springs and glue behavior remain.
Jaw caps stay rigid while the coil stretches; skeletal arms and separate cloth
fists follow the actual punch reach. Both the HUD preview and live hazards
use the same production art. No service equipment or arena prop image loads.

`familiars.ts` replaces courier drones with bats carrying wax-seal talismans.
Their wing cutouts hinge at the shoulders while the face stays rigid. The
original flight path, collectible center/radius, size variations, ownership
and pickup timing remain intact. Tiny-power labels retain a minimum readable
size. Pause fixes the entire carrier; reduced motion fixes wing flapping.

All four center obstacles retain their geometry and motion, with scratched
bone, mint, ochre and stitched-plum treatments. Menus, controls, instructions,
pause, results and debug controls use printed paper, ink borders, offset hard
shadows, condensed titles and typewriter labels from `src/styles/horror.css`.
The comic palette stays consistent in both system color schemes. Keyboard
focus, custom bindings, settings persistence and every control remain available.

The start menu uses a translucent midnight shell over the actual court, with
a 14-pixel backdrop blur and a lighter tint in place of the earlier opaque
scrim. Tilted control cards, the existing player-head atlas cells, a bat,
cloth fist, talisman and fire-skull stickers add the comic collage treatment
without generating or loading another illustration set. Match setup stays
alongside a persistent quick-controls guide on desktop; compact screens show
the guide first, with Start outside the scrolling area. Both players' five
bindings are visible on entry. `renderBindings` keeps these readouts and CPU
badges synchronized with the selected mode and key editor. Returning to the
menu resets scroll so the guide is visible again. The blur disappears during
play, and grain remains fixed at 16%.

## Scope and validation

People/Shaders and Arena/Beach/Rooftop controls are removed, including the HUD
location button. Their old storage keys are ignored, and the old player and
background modules are not imported into the active game. Earlier source art
and studies remain available as historical references. The active update now
covers the full visual presentation. Physics, audio and game rules remain
outside its scope.

Production build and TypeScript checks pass. Browser validation checks isolated
line boil, fixed sky/ground, subtle grain, deterministic frames, pause and
reduced motion, legacy preference handling, jump-serve, size powers, detached
limbs, responsive rendering, and asset errors. The current scene checks also
confirm a 2560-by-1600 plate, no raster scenery request, stationary lunar detail,
and very low pixel differences between contour variants. Results and screenshots
live in `output/horror-work/`; current background results are recorded in
`solid-forest-validation.json`.

Retina validation confirms a 2816-by-1760 game canvas for a 1408-by-880 CSS
court at device pixel ratio 2. A fully composed 3840-by-2400 background preview
is exported to `output/horror-work/procedural-background-3840.png`; the active
game continues to draw its scenery from code. The separate report is
`retina-background-validation.json`.

Historical grain-control browser checks covered animation, zero intensity,
increasing strength, synchronized controls, keyboard adjustment, persistence,
paused previews, reduced motion, mobile layout, and unavailable storage. Their
report, `output/horror-work/grain-controls-validation.json`, predates the fixed
16% setting and removal of the sliders.
The earlier grain-size and crosses check compared spatial noise correlation
across device pixel ratios before the solid forest revision. Its historical
report is `coarse-grain-crosses-validation.json`.

`full-comic-style-validation.json` records desktop/mobile screens, all four
obstacles, all nine prop loads, synchronized comic colors in dark mode and
stable paused prop frames. Screenshots include `comic-menu.png`,
`comic-results.png`, `comic-mobile.png`, `comic-classic.png` and the prop sheet
`comic-props.png`. Earlier background and grain-control checks also remain in
`output/horror-work/`.

`start-menu-validation.json` records control visibility on entry and return,
live remapping and reset, saved bindings, mode badges, image loads, translucent
blur styling, and working start/pause transitions. Viewports include
1440×1000, 1280×720, 768×1024, 390×844 and 360×640. Screenshots are saved as
`start-menu-desktop.png`, `start-menu-laptop.png`, `start-menu-390.png` and
`start-menu-360.png`.

The full existing suite has 160 passing tests and eight failures in power-up,
champion AI, and hazard physics checks. The same eight failures were reproduced
from a clean HEAD snapshot; no simulation or test files were changed. The
baseline result is saved in `output/horror-work/baseline-tests.json`.

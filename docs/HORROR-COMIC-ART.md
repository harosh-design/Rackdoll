# Pencil horror-comic art

The game shares one pencil horror-comic direction across three selectable maps.
Player 1 wears an ochre hat and coat, violet clothes, and an ink-patterned face covering.
Player 2 has exposed cream bones, a skull, and plum shorts. Graveyard contains
blue pencil-colored sky, a cream moon, bare branches at the edges, a solid black
forest skyline with small crosses, and a violet ground plane.

Crematorium restores the concept's domed brick furnaces, iron doors, valves,
corner arches and pipework, concentrated at the outer sides of a quiet steel-blue
wall. Small vector flames sit inside the painted firebox openings, wisps rise
beside the pipes, and two vector lamps sway gently from fixed ceiling anchors.
Its narrow central iron steam pipe pushes nearby players away; animated steam
stays inside the physical field's reach.

Dead Circus restores the striped roof, low distant benches, drums, a wagon wheel
and a crate around an open muted mauve backdrop. The side curtains are entirely
vector artwork: stripes, hatching, folds, torn hems, tie cords and tassels follow
the same slowly moving fabric shape. A sagging vector garland carries attached
bulbs and small pennants above the main play area. Its flexible center divider
is stitched plum fabric with patches; seams follow its six physical segments.

Both maps balance the concept illustrations with gameplay readability. Detailed
raster architecture and props frame the perimeter; the broad center behind the
players and the ball remains an even, subdued pencil field. Moving elements use
Canvas paths in `interiorMotion.ts`, with no animated bitmap frames or sprite
scenery. They share the scenery clock, freeze on pause and remain still with
reduced motion. Menu previews use the same scene at a fixed decorative pose.
Ash is limited to twelve faint motes, and dust to eight, to preserve ball visibility. Outdoor
weather and moon scenery appear only on Graveyard. Indoor particles and steam
freeze with the scenery clock on pause and remain still with reduced motion.

The menu's three Map cards render miniature views of the actual backgrounds and
their dividers. Selecting a card previews the complete court and fixes its
obstacle for every mode, restart and rematch. The browser remembers the map;
legacy center-obstacle choices migrate to the matching map. CPU difficulty
remains a separate setting.

## Assets and rig

Built-in ImageGen generated the player atlases, pencil plate and prop cutouts.
The working tree contains only the WebP assets loaded by the current game.
Historical source PNGs, generation prompts and packaging scripts are available
in earlier Git commits.

- `public/art/horror/background-moonlit.webp`: the quieter original sky/ground
  plate from the requested gameplay reference, packaged at 2560 by 1600.
  Its generated source is 1586 by 992; packaging registers and resamples it to
  the larger production size. Scenery is independently drawn at device resolution.
- `public/art/horror/background-crematorium.webp` and
  `background-dead-circus.webp`: illustrated static plates made with built-in
  ImageGen from the original concepts and the quiet gameplay color fields.
  Architecture and props are concentrated at the sides, with calm central walls.
  Lamps, flames, steam, hanging curtains, garlands and pennants are omitted from
  the raster art and rendered separately as vectors.
  Both source floor boundaries are registered at 84%, aligning the painted floor
  with the existing court geometry. Current prompts and raw plates are kept under
  ignored `output/map-concepts/balanced/`; previous versions remain in its parent.
- `public/art/horror/masked.webp`: thirteen masked-player cutouts in a 4-by-4 atlas.
- `public/art/horror/skeleton.webp`: thirteen skeleton cutouts with transparent rib gaps.
- `public/art/horror/props/*.webp`: eleven transparent comic prop cutouts for
  hazards, bats, talismans and fists, loaded by `src/render/horrorProps.ts`
  and `src/render/chameleonArt.ts`.
- `public/art/horror/balls/*.webp`: three transparent 512-pixel ball sprites,
  matching concepts 02 Stitched, 04 Dead Moon and 05 Evil Eye from the ball sheet.
  Built-in ImageGen isolated each selected concept; the sprites retain their
  coarse pencil texture, transparent margins and ink outlines.

Trees, distant forest, crosses and moon are generated by `src/render/horrorScenery.ts`.
The painted sky/ground boundary is normalized to 84% of the source image.
`src/render/horrorAtlas.json` maps cells into original local body
coordinates. The hat and coat tails have expanded visual bounds; this never
changes collision shapes. All thirteen parts use the existing closed-joint
poses, individual rotations, size powers, and detached-part scales. No opaque
skin undercoat or human silhouette mask is used behind skeleton bones.
The masked forearms are centered on their elbow pivots and extend 2.5 pixels
under the upper sleeves while keeping their wrist registration. Rounded elbow
ends reuse each sleeve's cloth texture to conceal the atlas's open black cuffs.

## Animation and paper texture

`HorrorScenery` builds a single solid forest skyline, bare branches entering
from the edges, and a cream lunar disc with ink crater patches and pencil
marks using seeded Canvas paths. It caches three contour variants at device
resolution, from 1280 to 3840 pixels wide. The scenery is clipped at the court's
drawn floor. Individual distant trees are removed: broad black masses form the
low horizon from the requested gameplay screenshot. Six small weathered crosses
protrude above that silhouette, with their bases hidden inside it.

The moon uses 32% drawing opacity and subdued crater marks so it blends into
the blue sky and stays behind the gameplay visually. Its center follows a
wide, shallow upper half-ellipse across the sky. Each crossing takes five
minutes, with a full return cycle of ten minutes. Cosine easing brings the moon
smoothly to rest at each edge before it retraces the same upper arc; it never
travels along the lower half. Three small cached moon cutouts move at the display refresh rate
behind the branches, independently of the scenery's contour redraws. The
orbit uses the scenery clock, freezes with the match, and stays still when
reduced motion is enabled.

Line boil is barely visible: at 5 Hz, only outline vertices move by at most
0.025 stage pixels per axis. Only the forest edge, foreground branches and moon
contour redraw. Crosses, crater details, bark marks and branch geometry stay
fixed within their own artwork; the lunar center moves only along its orbit.
This replaces the earlier raster displacement, which
moved internal texture. Blue sky, ground and collision boundaries remain fixed.

`PencilGrain` generates eight deterministic 256-pixel noise tiles once. A small
fixed soft-light pass supplies paper tooth; the dominant moving pass changes
tiles and offsets at 12 Hz. Each noise sample spans three CSS pixels, so the
coarser grain keeps the same visible size on ordinary and Retina displays.
Both opacities scale with the user's percentage:
0.1 times strength for fixed tooth and 0.52 times strength for animated grain,
where strength is normalized to 0–1. Frame rendering uses cached patterns and
two fills, without per-frame image readback, random-number generation, or a
WebGL dependency. Grain covers artwork before the HUD is painted, preserving
sharp interface text.

Grain intensity is fixed at 16% for both the court and its surrounding paper.
The main-menu and pause sliders, percentage readouts, and preference loading
have been removed. Earlier `rackdoll-grain-strength` values are ignored.
Painted pencil texture remains part of the underlying artwork.

The shared scenery clock stops while paused. Reduced motion fixes both the
line-boil frame and the moving grain pass. The same texture provides a
quiet matte surround outside the game canvas.

## Remaining game elements

`comicStyle.ts` supplies shared cream, ochre, mint, plum and rust spot colors,
fixed ink-panel contours, hatching and ragged impact bursts. `ballArt.ts` loads
the stitched ochre/plum leather, cratered cream moon and mint-eyed ball sprites.
At each match start, `Renderer.startMatch` randomly picks a skin other than the
previous match's and associates it with that world. Rounds, serves, pauses and
menu previews do not reroll it; restarting or replaying a match does. Art rotates
with the interpolated ball pose and fits its existing collision radius. Compact
Canvas versions preserve the selected design while loading or if an asset fails.
Each skin retains a subtle multiplying ownership tint that preserves ink detail.
Its actual flight history drives
three narrow speed strokes instead of
additive neon bloom. Charge, stun, power, impact and scoring feedback use the
same ink and lettering treatment. None of these passes changes collisions.

The five existing hazards now appear as a haunted magnet, voodoo boxer, fire
skull, bone-jaw spring trap and ectoplasm. Their existing IDs and lifetimes remain.
The boxer now aims its alternating fists at the head, and the fire skull
rotates toward its player independently of its flight direction, then turns
through one full somersault during the diving recovery after each dash.
The bone spring falls under gravity and redirects at physical bounces. Its
five overlapping inked coil loops bow and stretch with flight, compress on
surface contact, then release a damped wobble. The original textured jaw caps
hinge independently at the two ends. Plum coils gain ochre highlights and
longer paper-colored speed scratches as distance and impact strength build;
small directional ink marks show each surface rebound. The palette, existing
bone cutouts and ink outlines match the other props without digital bloom.
Skeletal arms and separate cloth
fists follow the actual punch reach. Both the HUD preview and live hazards
use the same production art. No service equipment or arena prop image loads.

The wall-crawling chameleon uses the approved Moss Wraith skin: sage scales,
fern and lichen markings, a cream belly, spiral tail and golden eye. Built-in
ImageGen prepared a separate leg-free body and one textured limb cutout in
`public/art/horror/props/chameleon-body.webp` and `chameleon-leg.webp`, with real
alpha transparency. Gameplay and the queue icon share the same articulated art.
The body is turned upright beside the wall, with four separate limbs driven by
two-segment inverse kinematics. Diagonal pairs alternate between gripping and
stepping. A planted toe pad stays at the actual wall face and at a fixed world
height while the body descends; a free foot lifts inward and reaches one stride
lower. Gait phase follows interpolated distance travelled, so stopping at the
floor or pausing also stops the feet. Emergence scales around the wall face to
keep the grips attached. Reduced motion fixes the limb pose. The iris follows
the target, and the rotated mouth remains the tongue's shared simulation anchor.
Canvas body and limb drawings provide a loading/error fallback.
The crawler moves at 23 world pixels per physics second and stops above the floor;
an attached tongue briefly pulls toward the mouth, sharing the player's impulse
across all surviving parts and the head rail to preserve the joints.

The two existing wall buttons use five side-view gravestone sprites under
`public/art/horror/graves/`: rounded, skull-seal, coffin, gothic and broken-seal.
Built-in ImageGen isolated the approved concepts and removed their stone
pedestals. All five retain their narrow side projection and contain no crosses.
`graveArt.ts` loads the cutouts and caches mint-active and silhouette-only flash
layers. The right wall mirrors its grave so both carved faces point inward.
At match start, each wall randomly receives a different design, excluding that
wall's previous match design. The pair stays fixed across rounds, serves and
pauses; menu previews do not consume random choices. Existing press, flash and
lifetime feedback remains. This is a visual skin: the original two colliders,
button positions, launch points and shared five-hazard queue are unchanged.

`familiars.ts` replaces courier drones with bats carrying wax-seal talismans.
Their wing cutouts hinge at the shoulders while the face stays rigid. The
original flight path, collectible center/radius, size variations, ownership
and pickup timing remain intact. Tiny-power labels retain a minimum readable
size. Pause fixes the entire carrier; reduced motion fixes wing flapping.

All three map dividers retain their existing geometry and motion, with scratched
bone, iron-and-steam, and stitched-plum treatments. Menus, controls, instructions,
pause, results and debug controls use printed paper, ink borders, offset hard
shadows, condensed titles and typewriter labels from `src/styles/horror.css`.
The comic palette stays consistent in both system color schemes. Keyboard
focus, custom bindings, settings persistence and every control remain available.

The start menu uses a translucent midnight shell over the actual court, with
a 14-pixel backdrop blur and a lighter tint in place of the earlier opaque
scrim. Tilted control cards, the existing player-head atlas cells, a bat,
cloth fist, talisman and fire-skull stickers add the comic collage treatment
without generating or loading another illustration set. Match setup stays
alongside a persistent quick-controls guide on desktop; compact screens show
the guide first, with Start outside the scrolling area. Both players' five
bindings are visible on entry. `renderBindings` keeps these readouts and CPU
badges synchronized with the selected mode and key editor. Returning to the
menu resets scroll so the guide is visible again. The blur disappears during
play, and grain remains fixed at 16%.

## Scope and validation

People/Shaders and the old Arena/Beach/Rooftop controls are removed, including the HUD
location button. The new Map selector replaces the independent center-obstacle
selector. Old player and location storage keys are ignored, and the old player and
background modules and their assets have been removed from the working tree.
Earlier source art and studies remain available in Git history. The active update
covers the full visual presentation. Physics, audio and game rules remain
outside its scope.

Run `npm run build` for TypeScript and production-build checks and `npm test`
for the simulation and input regression suite. Visual checks should cover line
boil, fixed sky/ground, grain, pause and reduced motion, jump-serve, size powers,
detached limbs, all three maps and all nine prop loads. Check the start menu
at desktop and mobile widths, including remapping, mode badges and start/pause
transitions. Save any new screenshots or validation reports under the ignored
`output/` directory.

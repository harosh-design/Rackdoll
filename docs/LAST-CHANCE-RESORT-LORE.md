# Last Chance Resort

## Premise

Last Chance Resort promises the perfect holiday: a tropical beach, attentive
robot staff, and an activity program that never ends. The trouble starts when
two tourists try to check out. The automated concierge refuses to issue their
return tickets until they complete the resort's mandatory beach volleyball
championship. Its hospitality software reads every request to leave as a
request for one more round of entertainment.

The guests enter the tournament to earn departure clearance. They are ordinary
holidaymakers, not trained athletes: awkward jumps, flailing limbs, and
accidental kicks are part of their escape. The beach remains beautiful, while
the staff turn a friendly match into an increasingly absurd holiday package.

## Story arc

The opening takes place at checkout. A cheerful concierge announces that the
guests' return tickets are locked behind the championship. They are directed
to the beach, where the wall-mounted service buttons supposedly call helpful
assistants and delivery drones distribute complimentary activity boosters.

The early matches teach the guests to play and to exploit the resort's rules.
A ball hitting an opponent's service button summons a troublesome assistant
on that side. A ball hitting a drone-delivered parcel earns its advertised booster. The same
machines that keep the guests on holiday become tools they can use to win.

As the championship advances, the resort advertises each harder opponent as a
premium upgrade. The final champion guards departure clearance. Winning the
championship forces the concierge to print the return tickets, even while it
tries to offer the guests another stay. The closing joke is a farewell gift
that is unmistakably another invitation to the court.

This arc is narrative direction. Checkout scenes, ticket presentation, and
dialogue are proposed story features; the existing game does not implement
them yet.

## Characters and tone

The two playable tourists wear recognizable holiday clothes: teal shorts for
one, a coral sports top and shorts for the other. Their personalities come
through physical reactions and determined improvisation. Neither needs a
military or superhero backstory.

The concierge is relentlessly polite and treats inconvenience as exceptional
service. The staff are malfunctioning hospitality machines, not murderous
villains. The tone is sunny absurdity with a clear goal: win the ticket home.
At night, warm hotel lights preserve the holiday atmosphere against a quieter,
cooler beach.

## Existing mechanics in the fiction

| Game element | Narrative role |
| --- | --- |
| Volleyball rounds and campaign opponents | Mandatory activities that earn departure clearance. |
| Articulated ragdoll movement, jumps, and charged flips | Untrained guests finding increasingly inventive ways to return the ball. |
| Wall service buttons | Requests for assistance that the ball can trigger on either side. |
| Magnet hazard | A luggage assistant that keeps pulling guests and loose equipment toward it. |
| Boxer hazard | An overenthusiastic fitness coach delivering an unwanted workout. |
| Comet hazard | An express porter whose high-speed service repeatedly knocks guests off course. |
| Spring hazard | A fitness assistant that turns every approach into a bouncing collision. |
| Slime hazard | A spa dispenser whose adhesive treatment tangles a guest's limbs. |
| Delivery drones and their visible gifts | Complimentary resort boosters; the attached gift advertises the effect before pickup. |
| Giant, tiny, feather ball, high jump, speed, magnet, and shield powers | Temporary activity upgrades from the resort's experimental recreation program. |
| HUD hazard queue | The concierge's preview of upcoming services. |

These interpretations give the existing rules a shared setting. They do not
change collision geometry, scoring, controls, power duration, or hazard behavior.

## Visual identity

The selected venue is a glass-domed indoor resort arena with structural ribs,
palms beyond the windows, and warm hospitality lights. The playable arena must be
easier to read than the scenery. Use a continuous visible floor boundary and
two straight wall faces; keep their positions tied to the physical surfaces.
Warm skin and saturated teal/coral clothing should stand out against a muted,
cool background.
